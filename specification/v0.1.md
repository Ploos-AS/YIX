## 1. Scope and terminology

µIX defines independently operated local or regional Internet exchange points. It does not define a new routing protocol or require a central service. The key words MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT and MAY are interpreted as described in BCP 14 (RFC 2119 and RFC 8174) when capitalized.

An exchange is an administrative peering domain; a site is a physical location; a participant is an independently administered network; a route server facilitates multilateral BGP peering without forwarding participant traffic.

## 2. Architecture

- ARCH-01: An exchange MUST provide a documented Ethernet peering fabric and MUST isolate management access from participant traffic.
- ARCH-02: An exchange MUST support IPv4 and IPv6 peering and MUST document addressing, MTU and port parameters.
- ARCH-03: An exchange MUST be independently operable without mandatory external µIX infrastructure or Ploos services.
- ARCH-04: Inter-site connectivity MAY be offered, but failure domains and operational responsibilities MUST be documented.
- ARCH-05: A single-switch deployment MAY be used if its lack of fabric redundancy is disclosed.

## 3. Participant access and routing

- PEER-01: Participants MUST be identified and authorized before activation; public Internet peering MUST use legitimately assigned routing resources.
- PEER-02: Bilateral BGP peering MUST be supported. Route-server participation MAY be offered and MUST be optional for participants.
- PEER-03: The operator MUST publish technical access criteria and apply equivalent conditions to equivalent participants without requiring purchase of unrelated services.
- PEER-04: Participant ports MUST enforce documented layer-2 protections and isolation from unauthorized traffic; the peering LAN MUST NOT provide an unintended default gateway or DHCP service.
- PEER-05: Participants MUST NOT advertise routes they are unauthorized to announce.

## 4. Route servers (conditional)

If route servers are offered:
- RS-01: They MUST implement documented route-server behavior consistent with RFC 7947 and MUST NOT forward participant data traffic.
- RS-02: They MUST enforce first-AS checks on participant imports unless a documented and validated exception applies, and MUST enforce per-participant prefix limits.
- RS-03: They MUST reject RPKI Invalid routes and MUST NOT treat NotFound as proof of authorization. They MUST document validation cache failure behavior.
- RS-04: They MUST enforce documented prefix authorization policy using appropriate verified routing data, and SHOULD automate filter updates.
- RS-05: They MUST prevent unintended route export and document supported communities and participant controls.
- RS-06: Redundant route servers SHOULD be deployed on independent failure domains. Any lack of redundancy MUST be disclosed.

## 5. Operations and security

- OPS-01: Operators MUST maintain participant contacts, incident procedures and change/rollback procedures.
- OPS-02: Operators MUST monitor fabric health and, where provided, route-server sessions, prefix counts and RPKI freshness.
- OPS-03: Administrative access MUST be authenticated, restricted and auditable; MFA SHOULD be used where supported.
- OPS-04: Configuration backups MUST be protected and restoration MUST be tested before production activation.
- OPS-05: Operators MUST publish actual service status and MUST NOT claim unverified redundancy, participants or availability commitments.

## 6. Conformance

A claim MUST identify the specification version, deployment profile (if defined), applicable conditional requirements, test evidence and unresolved deviations. All applicable MUST and MUST NOT requirements must be satisfied for a conformance claim; SHOULD deviations must be justified. Draft self-assessment is not independent certification. Physical reference implementations and lab fixtures MUST be distinguished.

Profiles, test procedures and certification governance remain future work. No existing deployment is declared conformant by this draft.
